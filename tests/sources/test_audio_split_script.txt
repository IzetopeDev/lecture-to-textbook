# Why Celeste is One of Gaming's Greatest Masterpieces

**[Opening - 0:00]**

In 2018, a small indie platformer quietly released on multiple platforms. It featured pixel art graphics, a simple premise about climbing a mountain, and cost just twenty dollars. This game was Celeste, developed by Maddy Makes Games, and it would go on to become one of the most critically acclaimed and beloved indie titles of the decade. But what makes Celeste so special? Why does this seemingly straightforward platformer resonate so deeply with millions of players worldwide? Today, we're going to explore exactly why Celeste isn't just a great game—it's a transformative experience that pushes the boundaries of what video games can achieve as an art form.

**[The Perfect Gameplay Foundation - 2:30]**

Let's start with what Celeste does best: pure, distilled platforming perfection. At its core, Celeste gives you an incredibly simple moveset. You can jump, dash once in mid-air, and climb walls briefly before your stamina runs out. That's it. No weapons, no power-ups, no gimmicks. Yet from these three simple mechanics, the game crafts over seven hundred screens of increasingly creative and challenging obstacles.

What makes the platforming exceptional is the precision and responsiveness of the controls. Every input feels exactly as intended. When you die—and you will die thousands of times—it never feels cheap or unfair. You know exactly why you failed, and more importantly, you know you can do better. The game respects your time with instant respawns and frequent checkpoints, turning what could be frustrating into a meditative flow state where you're constantly learning, adapting, and improving.

The difficulty curve is masterfully designed. Early chapters teach you the fundamentals, while later stages introduce mind-bending combinations of mechanics that seem impossible at first glance. Yet the game never gatekeeps its story behind skill barriers. If you're struggling, Assist Mode allows you to adjust game speed, grant infinite stamina, or even become invincible—all without judgment or locked content. This accessibility doesn't diminish the achievement of those who conquer the game unassisted; it simply acknowledges that everyone's relationship with challenge is different.

**[A Story That Matters - 5:45]**

But incredible gameplay alone doesn't make Celeste special. What elevates it to masterpiece status is how seamlessly it weaves its narrative about mental health, anxiety, and depression into every aspect of the experience. You play as Madeline, a young woman determined to climb Celeste Mountain despite having no mountaineering experience. It quickly becomes clear that this journey is as much internal as it is external.

The mountain serves as a perfect metaphor for personal struggle. It's imposing, dangerous, and seemingly insurmountable. Madeline battles not just environmental hazards, but also a dark reflection of herself—Part of You, or Badeline—who represents her anxiety, self-doubt, and negative thoughts. This manifestation actively works against her, creating obstacles and telling her she's not good enough, that she should give up.

What's revolutionary is how the game handles this conflict. Many stories about mental health position the "dark side" as something to be defeated or eliminated. Celeste takes a more nuanced, realistic approach. Through her journey, Madeline learns that Badeline isn't her enemy—she's a part of her that needs to be understood, accepted, and integrated. The game's emotional climax doesn't come from destroying her darkness, but from embracing it, working with it, and understanding that her anxiety and fears are valid parts of her identity.

This message resonates powerfully because it reflects genuine therapeutic approaches to mental health. You don't "win" against depression or anxiety—you learn to live with them, to understand their triggers, and to develop coping mechanisms. The game never trivializes mental illness or suggests it can be easily overcome, but it does offer hope that things can get better with perseverance, self-compassion, and sometimes, asking for help.

**[Audiovisual Excellence - 9:15]**

Celeste's presentation deserves special recognition. Lena Raine's soundtrack is nothing short of extraordinary, perfectly capturing the emotional tenor of each moment. From the melancholic piano of "First Steps" to the triumphant synths of "Reach for the Summit," the music doesn't just accompany the gameplay—it becomes an integral part of the emotional journey. Tracks like "Resurrections" during pivotal story moments have moved players to tears, while the intense electronic beats of later chapters perfectly match the adrenaline of difficult platforming sequences.

The pixel art, created primarily by Amora Bettany, is deceptively simple yet incredibly expressive. Character animations convey emotion through subtle details—Madeline's hunched posture when anxious, her determined expression when focused, the way she pants after difficult climbs. Environmental design ranges from serene mountain meadows to surreal dreamscapes, each area visually distinct and memorable. The game proves that you don't need photorealistic graphics to create stunning visual moments that stick with players long after they've finished.

**[Beyond the Summit - 11:30]**

For those who crave more challenge, Celeste offers B-Sides, C-Sides, and additional content that pushes platforming difficulty to extreme levels. These optional challenges are brutally hard, designed for players who want to truly master the game's mechanics. Yet even here, the game maintains its supportive philosophy. There's no shame in not completing these stages—they exist for those who want them, not as a barrier to feeling you've "really" beaten the game.

The Farewell DLC, released a year after the base game, adds another profound chapter to Madeline's story while introducing new mechanics and some of the most difficult platforming ever designed. It's a love letter to the community that formed around Celeste, and a meditation on letting go and moving forward.

**[Closing - 13:45]**

Celeste is amazing because it succeeds on every possible level. It's a precision platformer that rivals genre classics. It's a touching story about mental health that treats its subject matter with respect and authenticity. It's an audiovisual triumph that proves indie games can compete with any AAA production in artistic merit. It's accessible to newcomers while offering depth for hardcore players. Most importantly, it's a game that makes you feel something—hope, determination, catharsis.

In climbing Celeste Mountain alongside Madeline, players don't just overcome digital obstacles. They're reminded that their own mountains—whatever they may be—are worth climbing, one small step at a time. And sometimes, that message is exactly what someone needs to hear.

# The Sonic Ascent: Why Celeste's Soundtrack is a Masterclass in Video Game Music

**[Opening - 0:00]**

Close your eyes and imagine you're standing at the base of a mountain. The air is crisp, the path ahead uncertain, and you're about to embark on a journey that will test every fiber of your being. Now, imagine that journey has a soundtrack—not just background noise, but music that breathes with you, struggles with you, and ultimately triumphs with you. This is the power of Celeste's soundtrack, composed primarily by Lena Raine, with additional contributions from Kevin Regamey for the Farewell DLC. Today, we're going to dive deep into why this soundtrack isn't just good video game music—it's a transformative work of art that redefines how music and gameplay can intertwine to create unforgettable emotional experiences.

**[The Composer's Vision - 1:30]**

Lena Raine approached Celeste's soundtrack with a clear philosophy: the music needed to be as much a character in the game as Madeline herself. Having previously worked on titles like Guild Wars 2, Raine understood that video game music serves a unique purpose. It must enhance gameplay without overwhelming it, convey emotion without being heavy-handed, and remain engaging even after players hear it hundreds of times while attempting difficult sections.

What makes Raine's approach special is her willingness to break conventional video game music rules. Rather than creating safe, loopable background tracks that fade into obscurity, she crafted dynamic compositions that evolve, breathe, and respond to the player's journey. Each track tells its own story while contributing to the larger narrative arc of Madeline's ascent. The result is a soundtrack that players actively listen to, not just hear—one that has spawned countless covers, remixes, and concert performances.

Raine has been open about her own experiences with anxiety and depression, and this personal connection to the game's themes permeates every note. The music doesn't just accompany Madeline's mental health journey—it embodies it. You can hear the anxiety in the dissonant synths, the depression in the melancholic piano, and the hope in the soaring melodies. This authenticity is perhaps why the soundtrack resonates so deeply with players who've faced similar struggles.

**[First Steps and Foundations - 4:15]**

The soundtrack opens with "First Steps," and immediately establishes the emotional tone of the entire game. Built around a simple, repeating piano motif, the track feels simultaneously hopeful and uncertain. There's a childlike innocence to the melody, reflecting Madeline's naive determination as she begins her climb. But underneath, there's a subtle melancholy—a hint that this journey won't be as simple as she imagines.

What's brilliant about "First Steps" is how it introduces musical themes that will recur throughout the soundtrack. That piano melody becomes a leitmotif representing Madeline herself, appearing in various forms across different tracks. When you hear echoes of it in later, more intense compositions, it serves as a reminder of where the journey began and how far you've come. This kind of thematic consistency is something often reserved for film scores or orchestral works, yet Raine employs it masterfully in a platformer soundtrack.

The track also demonstrates Raine's understanding of gameplay pacing. It's energetic enough to keep players engaged during the learning curve of early levels, but calm enough not to stress them out as they learn the controls. The gentle electronic elements that support the piano give a modern, indie feel that perfectly matches the game's pixel art aesthetic. It's inviting, warm, and makes you want to keep climbing.

**[Ascending Into Anxiety - 7:00]**

As players progress through Chapter 2, they encounter "Resurrections," which marks the first major shift in the soundtrack's emotional palette. This track introduces more prominent electronic elements, pulsing synths that create a sense of urgency and unease. The piano from earlier chapters is still present, but now it's competing with these newer, harsher sounds—much like how Madeline's confidence must contend with emerging anxiety.

The genius of "Resurrections" is in its structure. It builds gradually, layering elements on top of each other until the soundscape feels almost claustrophobic. This mirrors both the gameplay—where obstacles become more numerous and complex—and Madeline's mental state as she encounters her reflection, Badeline. The track reaches moments of intense climax before pulling back, giving players brief respites that match the level design's checkpoint placement.

Listen carefully during the track's quieter moments, and you'll hear subtle glitches and distortions in the electronic elements. These aren't mistakes—they're intentional representations of Madeline's deteriorating mental state. The music itself is beginning to fracture, just as her psyche is under stress. This attention to detail, where sound design and composition blur together, shows a level of craft rarely seen in game music.

**[The Mirror Temple's Madness - 10:30]**

Chapter 5's "Mirror Temple (Mirror Magic Mix)" represents perhaps the most overtly intense track in the base game. Here, Raine fully embraces electronic and synth-heavy production, creating something that feels simultaneously retro and modern. Heavy bass lines drive the track forward with relentless energy, while arpeggiated synths create a dizzying, kaleidoscopic effect that matches the mirror-filled environment perfectly.

What makes this track exceptional is how it represents Badeline's influence. This is her domain—dark, chaotic, and overwhelming. The music is aggressive in a way previous tracks haven't been, with distorted sounds and dissonant harmonies that create genuine unease. Yet underneath all the chaos, you can still hear fragments of Madeline's theme trying to break through. It's a musical representation of internal conflict, where two parts of the same person struggle for dominance.

The track's structure is also fascinating from a gameplay perspective. It's intense enough to maintain tension during difficult platforming sequences, but it never becomes grating or anxiety-inducing to the point of distraction. This balance is incredibly difficult to achieve. Too calm, and the music wouldn't match the intensity of the gameplay. Too aggressive, and it would stress players out and make difficult sections even harder. Raine finds the sweet spot, creating music that energizes rather than exhausts.

**[Reconciliation and Reach - 13:45]**

The emotional and musical climax of the base game arrives with "Reach for the Summit," the Chapter 6 theme. This track represents everything the soundtrack has been building toward—a perfect synthesis of all the musical themes and emotional beats established throughout the journey. The piano returns, but now it's confident, supported rather than overwhelmed by the electronic elements. Driving percussion propels the track forward with unstoppable momentum, while soaring synthesizer leads create a sense of triumph and determination.

What's remarkable about "Reach for the Summit" is how it grows more powerful the more you've struggled to get there. Players who've died hundreds of times in earlier chapters, who've persevered through frustration and self-doubt, experience this track differently than someone who's breezed through on Assist Mode. The music feels earned. Every note celebrates not just Madeline's determination, but yours as the player. It's a perfect example of how video game music can create emotions impossible in passive media—the feeling of personal achievement amplified by perfect musical accompaniment.

The track also features one of Raine's most brilliant compositional choices: the callback to "First Steps." About halfway through "Reach for the Summit," you hear that original piano motif again, but now it's transformed—stronger, surer, supported by the full instrumentation the soundtrack has developed. It's a musical way of saying, "Look how far you've come," and it's devastatingly effective. Many players report getting emotional at this exact moment, and the music is a huge part of why.

**[B-Sides and Remixes - 17:00]**

Celeste's B-Side chapters feature remixed versions of the main stage themes, and these aren't mere afterthoughts—they're full reimaginings that often surpass the originals in complexity and emotional depth. Raine approached these remixes as opportunities to explore different musical directions while maintaining the core identity of each track.

The B-Side remixes are generally more intense and experimental than their counterparts, matching the increased difficulty of these optional challenges. "Old Site (Night Mix)," for example, takes the already eerie Chapter 2 theme and pushes it into darker, more ambient territory. The result is genuinely unsettling, perfect for the harder platforming challenges that await.

What's interesting is how these remixes serve players differently than main themes. Because B-Sides are optional and extremely difficult, players will hear these tracks many, many times—potentially for hours as they attempt the same difficult screens repeatedly. Raine designed them with this repetition in mind, ensuring they remain engaging even after the hundredth listen. This requires careful balance in the composition—enough variation to stay interesting, but not so much dynamic change that it becomes distracting during precision platforming.

**[Farewell's Evolution - 20:15]**

When the Farewell DLC released in 2019, it brought with it new music from both Lena Raine and Kevin Regamey. These tracks represent an evolution of Celeste's musical identity while introducing fresh ideas. Raine's "wavedash.ppt" for Chapter 9 is particularly noteworthy—it's simultaneously nostalgic and forward-looking, melancholic and determined.

The Farewell soundtrack had to accomplish something difficult: provide closure to Madeline's journey while acknowledging that some struggles continue. Tracks like "The Woman and the Bird" achieve this beautifully, combining the familiar piano themes with new melodic ideas that suggest growth and acceptance rather than complete resolution. The music understands that mental health isn't about reaching a finish line—it's about the ongoing journey.

Kevin Regamey's contributions bring a slightly different flavor while remaining cohesive with Raine's established musical language. His work on tracks like "Beyond the Heart" showcases his ability to create intense, driving electronic music that pushes players forward through some of the game's most challenging platforming. The collaboration between the two composers feels natural, each bringing their strengths while respecting the soundtrack's overall vision.

**[Technical Brilliance - 23:00]**

From a technical standpoint, Celeste's soundtrack showcases impressive production quality. The mixing is immaculate—every element has its place in the soundscape, nothing fights for attention or muddles the overall sound. The bass is punchy without being overwhelming, the highs are crisp without being harsh, and the midrange carries the melodic content with clarity.

The use of synthesis is particularly noteworthy. Raine employs a variety of synth techniques—from warm, analog-style pads to aggressive digital leads—creating a rich sonic palette that never feels repetitive. The way she layers these sounds, building complex textures from simple elements, demonstrates both technical skill and artistic vision.

The soundtrack also makes clever use of dynamic range. Quieter sections allow players to breathe and think, while louder passages create intensity during challenging gameplay moments. This ebb and flow mirrors both good level design and natural emotional rhythms, creating a holistic experience where music, gameplay, and narrative feel inseparable.

**[Cultural Impact and Legacy - 25:30]**

Beyond the game itself, Celeste's soundtrack has achieved remarkable cultural penetration. It's been performed at video game music concerts worldwide, covered by countless musicians across every genre imaginable, and has introduced many people to the possibilities of video game music as serious artistic expression.

The soundtrack's popularity on streaming platforms is notable—tracks like "Resurrections" and "Reach for the Summit" have millions of plays, with listeners including both players and people who've never touched the game. This crossover appeal speaks to the music's quality as standalone compositions, not just functional game audio.

Perhaps most importantly, the soundtrack has become comfort music for many people dealing with mental health struggles. The comments on YouTube uploads of Celeste tracks are filled with people sharing how the music helps them through difficult times, how it reminds them they're not alone, how it gives them strength to keep trying. This is the ultimate achievement for any artist—creating work that genuinely helps people.

**[Closing - 28:00]**

Celeste's soundtrack is a masterclass in video game composition because it understands that music in games isn't just decoration—it's an essential component of the interactive experience. Lena Raine and Kevin Regamey created something that enhances gameplay, deepens narrative, and stands alone as exceptional music in its own right. Every note serves a purpose, every melodic choice reinforces the themes of struggle, acceptance, and hope that define Madeline's journey.

The soundtrack proves that video game music has evolved beyond its roots, becoming a legitimate art form capable of moving people as profoundly as any symphony or song. It's music that makes you feel seen, understood, and capable of climbing your own mountains. And really, what more could you ask from a soundtrack than that?
